\title{MIST: A Multi-Context Spatial Transformer for Gene Expression Prediction from Histology}

\author{Anonymous}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Spatial transcriptomics reveals how gene expression is organized across a tissue; however, it is expensive and time-consuming. This motivates predicting gene expression from standard H\&E images. A spot's morphology, its spatial neighborhood, and the broader tissue context provide complementary information for predicting gene expression from H\&E images. A key modeling challenge is how to integrate these cues effectively. We introduce \morph{}, a compact spatial transformer that predicts a slide's spatial gene expression in a single forward pass by fusing three complementary views of context in every layer: local attention over spatial nearest neighbors, global self-attention across all spots, and an attention-pooled slide representation. Because the model uses spot coordinates only through pairwise distances, its predictions are invariant to translations, rotations, and reflections of the coordinate frame when image features are fixed. On six human organ cohorts from HEST-1k, evaluated under intra-cohort, pooled multi-organ, and leave-one-organ-out settings, \morph{} attains the highest mean Pearson correlation (PCC) on every cohort, improving over the strongest baseline by $9.7\%$, $11.6\%$ and $11.4\%$ for the 10-, 50-, and 100-gene panels, respectively, with the lowest MSE and MAE. Moreover, the gains are statistically significant and persist across the evaluated gene-selection strategies (HVG, DEG, and HMHVG) and encoder features (UNI, CONCH, and UNI+CONCH). It generalizes to held-out organs ($+6.0\%$ over the closest baseline BLEEP). On 58 held-out slides, MIST outperformed BLEEP in spatial-domain recovery on 78\% of slides according to ARI and on 90\% according to NMI. It also reproduces spatial expression patterns of selected melanoma-associated genes (mean PCC $0.90$) and breast cancer receptor genes associated with HER2, ER, and PR, supporting its ability to capture biologically relevant molecular patterns from H\&E images.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Spatial transcriptomics (ST) measures gene expression together with tissue location, enabling the
study of spatial heterogeneity, tissue organization, and cellular interactions
\cite{staahl2016visualization}. Its cost and experimental burden, however, constrain the number and
diversity of specimens that can be profiled. In contrast, H\&E slides are routinely collected and
contain morphological signals associated with tissue composition and molecular state
\cite{chen2024stimage,jaume2024hest}. Predicting spatial gene expression from these images could
therefore extend molecular analysis to specimens without matched ST measurements
\cite{he2020stnet,zeng2022hist2st,xie2023bleep}.

Morphology and expression interact locally, across the slide, and at the level of slide composition. Existing predictors capture parts of this structure with patch regression, spatial graphs, retrieval, or conditional generation~\cite{he2020stnet,zeng2022hist2st,xie2023bleep,zhu2025diffusion}, but absolute coordinates can make predictions depend on an arbitrary scanner frame. Geometric representations based on relative quantities provide a principled way to reduce this dependence~\cite{satorras2021egnn}. We therefore seek a compact predictor that combines multi-scale context while using coordinates only through relative distances.

We introduce \morph{}, a direct spatial transformer that represents each spot as a token and combines spatial $k$NN attention for local context, full self-attention for slide-wide context, and attention pooling for a slide summary~\cite{vaswani2017attention}. Because spot coordinates enter the model only through pairwise distances, its predictions are invariant to translations, rotations, and reflections when image features are fixed (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Main idea.}
MIST predicts spatial gene expression by combining three complementary
messages at every spot: distance-aware self-attention over nearby spots,
standard global self-attention over all spots, and an attention-pooled
summary of the overall tissue composition.
\end{keybox}

We evaluate \morph{} across six human cohorts under intra-cohort prediction, pooled multi-organ prediction, and leave-one-organ-out transfer. In intra-cohort evaluation, \morph{} achieves the highest mean Pearson correlation and the lowest mean squared error (MSE) and mean absolute error (MAE), averaged across cohorts, for all evaluated gene-selection strategies and panel sizes. It also improves transfer to held-out organs and preserves biologically meaningful spatial patterns for melanoma- and breast-cancer-associated genes. Finally, on 58 held-out slides, \morph{} improves spatial-domain recovery over BLEEP on 78\% of slides according to ARI and on 90\% according to NMI, although absolute domain agreement remains modest.

\noindent Our experiments address four research questions:
\textbf{RQ1 (Performance):} How accurately does \morph{} predict gene expression across organs when trained separately per cohort or jointly across cohorts?
\textbf{RQ2 (Transfer):} How well does it generalize to an organ excluded from training?
\textbf{RQ3 (Design):} How do the image encoder, context pathways, and neighborhood construction affect predictive accuracy?
\textbf{RQ4 (Structure):} How well do predicted expression profiles preserve spatial domains identified from measured expression, as measured by ARI and NMI?

To sum up, we have the following summarized contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item We introduce \morph{}, a compact multi-context spatial transformer that integrates local neighborhood attention, global slide-wide attention, and slide-level context using continuous pairwise-distance features and coordinate-frame invariance.
  \item We establish a controlled evaluation protocol spanning intra-cohort, pooled cross-organ, and held-out-organ settings, with matched gene panels, image features, splits, baselines, and model selection.
  \item We show that \morph{} achieves strong predictive performance across organs, gene-selection strategies, panel sizes, and encoder features, while remaining a deterministic single-pass predictor.
  \item We evaluate downstream biological structure, showing improved spatial-domain recovery according to ARI and NMI and accurate reconstruction of cancer-associated marker-gene patterns.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\noindent\textbf{H\&E-to-ST prediction.}\quad Existing methods include patch-level regression
(ST-Net~\cite{he2020stnet}), spatial graph models (HisToGene~\cite{pang2021histogene};
Hist2ST~\cite{zeng2022hist2st}), cross-modal retrieval (BLEEP~\cite{xie2023bleep}), and
multi-resolution or multi-cell approaches (TRIPLEX~\cite{chung2024triplex};
M2OST~\cite{wang2025m2ost}). STEM~\cite{zhu2025diffusion} instead models expression
conditionally with diffusion. Because published results use different features, panels, and splits,
we compare these model families under a matched protocol; \morph{} remains a deterministic point
predictor rather than a distributional model.

\noindent\textbf{Context modeling.}\quad Spatial predictors differ in how they aggregate context:
some use only local patches, whereas others add neighboring-spot graphs, nonlocal image information,
or multi-resolution features. \morph{} combines distance-aware attention over local neighbors,
standard self-attention over all spots, and an attention-pooled tissue summary in every layer.

\noindent\textbf{Pathology foundation models.}\quad Recent pathology encoders provide transferable
representations for morphology and tissue composition~\cite{chen2024uni,lu2024conch}. We use frozen
UNI and CONCH features as matched inputs for \morph{} and the baselines, and separately evaluate
encoder choice to distinguish representation quality from architectural effects.

\noindent\textbf{Geometry and generalization.}\quad Absolute position encodings can change when an
equivalent tissue layout is translated, rotated, or reflected; geometric networks instead construct
interactions from relative quantities~\cite{satorras2021egnn}. \morph{} uses pairwise Euclidean
distances, which are invariant to these frame changes for fixed histology features. We also separate
pooled cross-validation, where organs remain represented in training, from leave-one-organ-out
(LOOO) evaluation on HEST-1k~\cite{jaume2024hest} to measure anatomical transfer. Further positioning
details appear in Appendix~\ref{app:related}.

\section{Method}

MIST maps frozen spot-level histology features and measured spot coordinates to a spatial gene
expression map. Its method has three stages: coordinate-invariant distance encoding, a shared
multi-context layer, and supervised prediction with a correlation-aware objective.

\subsection{Problem Setup}
For a slide with $N$ spots, let
$\mathbf{F}=[\mathbf{f}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{f}_N]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$
denote frozen pathology features and
$\mathbf{P}=[\mathbf{p}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{p}_N]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times2}$ the corresponding
spot coordinates. The target
$\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times G}$ contains log-normalized expression for $G$ genes. We learn
$f_\theta:(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P})\mapsto\hat{\mathbf{Y}}$ and process each slide independently;
the model therefore supports a variable number of spots and does not require expression measurements at
test time. In the feature-matched main comparisons, $\mathbf{f}_i$ concatenates frozen UNI
\cite{chen2024uni} and CONCH \cite{lu2024conch} embeddings ($d_{\mathrm{in}}=1536$).


\subsection{Coordinate-Invariant Distance Encoding}
We first compute the pairwise Euclidean distance matrix
$D_{ij}=\lVert\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j\rVert_2$. For each spot $i$, we retain the indices
$\mathcal{N}(i)$ and distances of its $k$ nearest neighbors, excluding the spot itself. Distances
are expanded into $M$ radial-basis features,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\phi}(D_{ij})=
\left[\exp\!\left(-\gamma_m D_{ij}^{2}\right)\right]_{m=1}^{M},
\end{equation}
where the fixed bandwidth parameters $\gamma_m$ are logarithmically spaced. These features influence
attention weights but are never added to the spot embeddings as absolute positions.

\par\noindent\refstepcounter{proposition}\textbf{Proposition \theproposition\ (Coordinate frame invariance):}\label{prop:coordinate_invariance}\par
For fixed model parameters and fixed histology features, in deterministic evaluation mode, let
$R\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ be orthogonal, let $\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$, and define
$\mathbf{P}'=\mathbf{P}R^\top+\mathbf{1}\mathbf{t}^\top$. If the $k$NN construction resolves distance
ties consistently, the prediction is unchanged:
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}')=f_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}).
\end{equation}
Details, qualifications, and the proof of this proposition are provided in
\hyperref[app:formal_guarantees]{Supplementary Section~B}.


\subsection{Multi-Context MIST Layer}
\insightbox{Core Idea.}{At every layer, each spot receives three messages in parallel: one from its
nearby spots, one from all spots, and one from a pooled summary of the slide. The model adds these
messages to the current spot representation and refines the result with a feed-forward network.
Only the nearby-spot message uses coordinates, and it uses pairwise distances rather than absolute
positions.}

The input features are projected to hidden width $d$,
$\mathbf{X}^{(0)}=\mathbf{F}W_{\mathrm{in}}+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{in}}$. The same neighbor graph and
radial-basis features are reused in all $L$ transformer layers. At layer $\ell$, a pre-normalized
representation $\mathbf{H}=\operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)})$ is passed through three context
pathways.

\subsubsection{Local spatial-neighborhood attention}
Each spot attends to its $k$ neighbors with a
learned, distance-dependent bias for each attention head. For head $h$, the attention weights are
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij}^{h}=\operatorname{softmax}_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\!\left(
\frac{(\mathbf{q}_i^{h})^\top\mathbf{k}_j^{h}}{\sqrt{d_h}}
+b_h\!\left(\boldsymbol{\phi}(D_{ij})\right)\right),
\end{equation}
where $d_h=d/H$ for $H$ heads. The local output is the concatenation of the weighted value sums,
followed by a learned output projection. Morphological similarity is captured by the query--key
term, while spatial distance can contribute through the learned radial-basis bias. In the reported
native-coordinate HEST configuration, neighbor distances are large enough that the RBF values are
negligible, so the main practical contribution of this pathway is attention over the spatial $k$NN
neighborhood; the continuous-distance bias is evaluated in the corresponding ablations.

\subsubsection{Global self-attention}
In parallel, standard multi-head self-attention
\cite{vaswani2017attention} allows every spot to attend to all $N$ spots. This pathway captures
long-range dependencies that may not be connected in the local spatial graph. It introduces no
direct coordinate-dependent bias and is permutation-equivariant with respect to spot order; in later
layers, its inputs may nevertheless carry spatial information propagated by the local and
slide-context pathways.

\subsubsection{Attention-pooled slide context}
A learned scalar scoring function produces
$a_i=\operatorname{softmax}_{i}(s(\mathbf{h}_i))$. The slide representation is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g}=W_g\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_i\mathbf{h}_i\right)+\mathbf{b}_g,
\end{equation}
and is broadcast to every spot. Unlike a learned class token, this representation is computed
directly from the slide's current spot representations and summarizes the slide-level context.

For the complete model, the local ($\mathbf{L}$), global ($\mathbf{G}$), and broadcast slide
($\mathbf{S}$) representations are summed and added to the residual stream. A second
pre-normalization residual update applies a two-layer feed-forward network with expansion factor four
and GELU activation:
\begin{align}
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}
  &=\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{L}^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{G}^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{S}^{(\ell)},\\
\mathbf{X}^{(\ell+1)}
  &=\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}+
    \operatorname{FFN}\!\left(\operatorname{LN}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)})\right).
\end{align}
After $L$ layers, layer normalization and a linear head map each spot representation to its $G$
predicted gene values. The model produces the complete slide prediction in one forward pass.

\par\noindent\refstepcounter{proposition}\textbf{Proposition \theproposition\ (Permutation equivariance):}\label{prop:permutation_equivariance}\par
Let $Q\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}$ be a permutation matrix. If the $k$NN construction handles distance
ties equivariantly, then
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(Q\mathbf{F},Q\mathbf{P})=Qf_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}).
\end{equation}
Therefore, reordering the input spots only reorders their predicted expression profiles.
\hyperref[app:formal_guarantees]{Supplementary Section~B} provides the proof. Our numerical checks evaluate both
Propositions~\ref{prop:coordinate_invariance} and~\ref{prop:permutation_equivariance} under finite
precision.


\subsection{Training Objective and Ablations}
\label{sec:loss}
For a slide with measured expression $\mathbf{Y}$, we optimize mean squared error together with a
gene-wise Pearson-correlation term:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{MSE}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}},\mathbf{Y})
+ \lambda\left(1-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{V}}\rho_g\right),
\end{equation}
where $\rho_g$ is the Pearson correlation between predicted and measured expression across the $N$
spots, $\mathcal{V}$ is the set of genes with nonzero target variance on that slide, and
$\lambda=0.5$. MSE constrains the scale of the predictions, while the correlation term encourages
the model to reproduce spot-to-spot expression patterns.

The complete model uses all three context pathways. Their individual contributions are evaluated in
the factorial ablation study reported in Section~\ref{sec:design}, which compares local (L), global
(G), and slide-level (S) context configurations.






\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\noindent\textbf{Data and preprocessing.}\quad
We evaluate on \textbf{HEST-1k}~\cite{jaume2024hest} using frozen UNI+CONCH
spot features (1536 dimensions) and $\log(1+x)$-transformed expression targets.
The cross-organ analyses use six cohorts: CCRCC, IDC, LUNG, PAAD, PRAD, and
SKCM; HCC, LYMPH\_IDC, and READ are excluded. Additional cohort details are
provided in Appendix~\ref{app:cohort_stats}.

\noindent\textbf{Gene-panel selection.}\quad
Unless otherwise stated, the default target is the 50-gene HEST HVG panel,
whose genes are selected by highest expression variance. To test sensitivity
to gene-selection strategy, we also evaluate 50-gene DEG and HMHVG panels. DEG
genes are selected using differential expression across Leiden clusters,
whereas HMHVG combines mean-expression and variability criteria after variance
filtering (Table~\ref{tab:panel_strategy}). We additionally evaluate panel
sizes of 10, 50, and 100 genes.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation regimes.}\quad
We consider intra-cohort training, in which a separate model is trained for
each cohort; pooled cross-organ training, in which slide-level samples are
held out while organ types remain represented in training; and leave-one-organ-
out (LOOO) transfer, in which the test organ is absent from training. Organ
identity is not provided as a model input.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics and validation.}\quad
Our primary metric is mean per-gene Pearson correlation (PCC). We also report
MSE and MAE for expression-scale accuracy, and ARI and NMI for spatial-domain
recovery. Efficiency is measured using inference latency and peak GPU memory.
Checkpoints are selected on inner validation folds and evaluated once on the
outer test fold; results are averaged over three seeds.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines and implementation.}\quad
ST-Net~\cite{he2020stnet}, Hist2ST~\cite{zeng2022hist2st}, BLEEP~\cite{xie2023bleep},
and STEM~\cite{zhu2025diffusion} are evaluated under matched features, panels,
splits, budgets, and model-selection rules. Unless otherwise stated, \morph{}
uses four layers, width 256, eight spatial neighbors, and 4.88M trainable
parameters. Full preprocessing, split, hyperparameter, and implementation
details are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:extended_setup}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}


\subsection{RQ1: Predictive performance across diverse organs}
\label{sec:rq1}
We begin by asking whether \morph{} predicts spatial expression accurately on organs it has
\emph{seen} during training. To answer this, we measure accuracy in two complementary ways under
matched features, panels, and splits: a separate model trained \emph{per cohort} (intra-cohort), and a
\emph{single} model trained jointly across all organs (pooled).

In the intra-cohort setting, \morph{} is best under every 50-gene selection strategy
(Table~\ref{tab:panel_strategy}), with mean PCCs of $0.496$ (HVG), $0.615$ (DEG), and $0.650$
(HMHVG), compared with BLEEP's $0.444$, $0.559$, and $0.587$. The advantage also holds across panel
sizes (Table~\ref{tab:main}): \morph{} reaches PCC@10/50/100 values of $0.600/0.496/0.559$ and wins
all six cohorts at PCC@50, yielding an $11.6\%$ gain over BLEEP. It also has the lowest MSE and MAE
under every tested strategy and panel size (Table~\ref{tab:error_strategy}).

The intra-cohort improvement is statistically reliable: across six cohorts and three seeds
($n{=}18$ paired runs), the PCC@50 gain over BLEEP is $0.051$ (95\% bootstrap CI
$[0.041,0.062]$; Wilcoxon $p{=}7.6\times10^{-6}$). At the per-gene level, \morph{} outperforms
BLEEP on $87\%$ of genes ($n{=}300$; Wilcoxon $p<10^{-30}$). Full paired tests are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:significance}.

\subsection{RQ2: Cross-organ transfer to an unseen organ}
\label{sec:transfer}
Having established strong performance on \emph{seen} organs, we now ask the harder question of whether
\morph{} transfers to an organ it has \emph{never} seen. To test this, we use leave-one-organ-out
(LOOO) evaluation: we train on all-but-one organ and test on the held-out one, so the target anatomy is
entirely absent from training. As expected, accuracy falls relative to the pooled setting of RQ1, yet \morph{} still transfers best:
on the six-organ subset it wins \emph{every} held-out organ and attains the highest mean PCC
($0.422$ vs.\ $0.398$ for BLEEP; a significant gain, Wilcoxon $p{=}0.018$, Appendix~\ref{app:significance}).
The complete per-cohort results for all six organs are reported in Table~\ref{tab:looo_6organ}; on the
six-organ HEST panel \morph{} reaches $0.422$ versus
$0.398$ for BLEEP, the strongest baseline on the six-organ panel. Together,
these results show that \morph{}'s inductive biases carry over to unseen anatomy, which supports RQ2; a
complementary mouse experiment in Appendix~\ref{app:mouse} shows the same trend.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Key Finding (RQ2):} The pooled$\to$LOOO drop, from $0.599$ with the organ seen (RQ1) to
$0.422$ with the organ held out, isolates unseen-organ transfer as the dominant remaining gap;
predicting seen organs well does not by itself transfer to a new organ.
\end{keybox}

\label{sec:design}

Three findings merit emphasis. First, a compact direct predictor is competitive when features,
gene panels, splits, and model selection are controlled; the $11.6\%$ relative gain over the
strongest baseline rests on matched experimental conditions, not a larger model. Second, pooled
multi-organ performance is not a substitute for unseen-organ evaluation: the drop from $0.599$
(pooled) to $0.422$ (LOOO) shows that anatomical shift remains a separate and harder problem. Third,
local spatial-neighborhood attention is the dominant contributor in the factorial study; global and
slide pathways provide moderate complementary gains, and the complete model (L\,+\,G\,+\,S) achieves
the best mean on the pooled evaluation.

Direct and generative models serve different purposes. STEM \cite{zhu2025diffusion} models a
conditional expression distribution and can represent one-to-many uncertainty through iterative
sampling. \morph{} produces a point estimate optimized for correlation and squared error. Its
benefits, including deterministic, single-pass inference and explicit coordinate invariance, do not
imply that morphology uniquely determines expression.

\textbf{Limitations.} The evaluation predicts $50$ genes from frozen UNI+CONCH embeddings; results
may not extend to transcriptome-scale output or end-to-end image encoding. LOOO uses a fixed
benchmark panel and does not test platform or institution shift simultaneously. Coordinate invariance
holds only for the spatial pathway with fixed image features; rotating the histology image can change
its frozen embedding. Domain-recovery analysis covers only two baselines and has high slide-level
variance. Pooled folds are sample-disjoint but cannot be verified as patient-disjoint where patient
identifiers are unavailable.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
We developed \morph{}, a coordinate-invariant local--global spatial transformer for predicting gene
expression from histology. Pairwise-distance encoding makes the spatial pathway invariant to rigid
coordinate transformations by construction, while three parallel context signals: local $k$NN
attention, global self-attention, and slide-level pooling, together produce complete-slide
predictions in a single pass. These design choices directly address our design goals that
multi-scale context is necessary and that architectural positional bias should not break under
coordinate shift. Under matched HEST-1k evaluation, \morph{} improves mean intra-cohort correlation
and remains competitive in LOOO transfer; the pooled-to-LOOO gap exposes the remaining difficulty of
anatomical transfer as the central open challenge.

For future work, it would be valuable to explore how conditioning on slide-level metadata (organ
type, sequencing platform) can narrow this anatomical gap, for instance, through FiLM or
adaptive layer normalisation conditioners that are available at inference time without requiring
expression labels. Scaling the gene panel beyond 50 genes, incorporating end-to-end image encoding,
and combining \morph{}'s geometric structure with calibrated generative uncertainty modeling
(as in STEM) are further natural directions.

\section{Formal Guarantees}
\label{app:formal_guarantees}

This section gives the detailed statements and proofs of the coordinate-frame invariance and
permutation-equivariance properties used in the main text. The guarantees concern deterministic
evaluation with fixed model parameters and fixed histology features. In finite-precision computation,
negligible round-off differences may occur; nearest-neighbor distance ties must also be resolved
consistently.

\subsection{Coordinate-frame invariance}

\textbf{Proposition 1 (Coordinate frame invariance).}
Let $R\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ be orthogonal and let $\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$. For
$\mathbf{P}'=\mathbf{P}R^\top+\mathbf{1}\mathbf{t}^\top$, the pairwise distances satisfy
$D'_{ij}=D_{ij}$. Consequently, for fixed histology features $\mathbf{F}$,
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}')=f_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}).
\end{equation}

\textit{Proof.}
For any two spots,
\begin{align}
\mathbf{p}'_i-\mathbf{p}'_j
  &= (\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j)R^\top,\\
\|\mathbf{p}'_i-\mathbf{p}'_j\|_2^2
  &= (\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j)R^\top R(\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j)^\top
   = \|\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j\|_2^2.
\end{align}
Thus, the full distance matrix and the distance-based $k$NN ordering are unchanged, assuming
consistent tie handling. Any optional median-distance normalization is also unchanged because its
input distances are unchanged, and therefore the RBF features $\boldsymbol{\phi}(D_{ij})$ are
unchanged. The local attention receives the same neighbor indices and distance features, while the
global attention, slide pooling, residual blocks, and prediction head receive the same histology
features and intermediate representations. Induction over the transformer layers gives identical
outputs. The result does not assert invariance of the frozen image encoder to transformations of the
H\&E image itself. $\square$

\subsection{Permutation equivariance}

\textbf{Proposition 2 (Permutation equivariance).}
Let $Q\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}$ be a permutation matrix. If the $k$NN construction resolves distance
ties equivariantly, then
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(Q\mathbf{F},Q\mathbf{P})=Qf_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}).
\end{equation}

\textit{Proof.}
Permuting the rows of $\mathbf{P}$ permutes the rows and columns of its pairwise distance matrix.
With equivariant tie handling, the neighbor list and corresponding distances are permuted in the
same way. Hence each local attention computation is permuted with its associated spot. The global
self-attention operation is permutation-equivariant because queries, keys, and values are permuted
together. Attention pooling produces the same pooled vector, since it is a weighted sum over the
spots, while broadcasting that vector preserves the row permutation. The input projection, residual
addition, layer normalization, feed-forward network, and final per-spot prediction head are all
row-wise equivariant. Applying these observations inductively through all layers yields
$f_\theta(Q\mathbf{F},Q\mathbf{P})=Qf_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P})$. $\square$

\section{Extended Related Work}
\label{app:related}

\paragraph{Machine-learning prediction of gene expression from histology.}
ST-Net~\cite{he2020stnet} established patch-level regression. Hist2ST~\cite{zeng2022hist2st} adds
spatial context through a graph over neighboring spots, while BLEEP~\cite{xie2023bleep} aligns image
and expression embeddings for cross-modal retrieval. STEM~\cite{zhu2025diffusion} instead treats
expression prediction as conditional generation. These methods use different features, gene panels,
splits, and selection procedures, so their published scores do not isolate architectural gains; our
comparisons therefore use a matched protocol. \morph{} is a deterministic point predictor and does
not model the full conditional expression distribution.

\paragraph{Geometric structure and coordinate invariance.}
Spatial prediction depends on the coordinate representation supplied to a model. Absolute position
embeddings can encode location within a particular coordinate frame, but their output may change when
an equivalent tissue layout is translated, rotated, or reflected. Geometric networks address this by
constructing interactions from relative quantities~\cite{satorras2021egnn}. \morph{} uses pairwise
Euclidean distances, which are preserved by translations, rotations, and reflections; the guarantee
applies to the spatial pathway for fixed histology features, not to rotations of the image-encoder
input.

\paragraph{Generalization across samples and organs.}
HEST-1k~\cite{jaume2024hest} spans many cohorts and organs. Within-cohort evaluation can exploit
organ-specific morphology or acquisition effects. We therefore distinguish pooled cross-validation,
which holds out samples while retaining their organ types in training, from LOOO, which withholds an
entire organ and provides a stricter test of anatomical transfer.

\paragraph{Positioning of \morph{}.}
\morph{} connects these three directions: an inductive-bias-explicit spatial transformer for direct
regression, evaluated under a matched cross-cohort protocol. Unlike absolute position encodings or
frame-averaging strategies, its coordinate pathway is invariant by construction. Unlike generative
models, it does not represent expression uncertainty; its advantage is deterministic, single-pass
inference with explicit geometric structure. Unlike prior evaluations that report within-cohort
accuracy only, our controlled comparison separates interpolation from anatomical transfer, making the
difficulty of organ shift directly measurable.

\section{Extended Experimental Setup}
\label{app:extended_setup}

\paragraph{Dataset and preprocessing.}
We evaluate on HEST-1k \cite{jaume2024hest} using the six cohorts listed in
Table~\ref{tab:cohort_stats}: CCRCC, IDC, LUNG, PAAD, PRAD, and SKCM. We use the
provided patient-stratified folds, $\log(1+x)$ normalization, and frozen UNI+CONCH features (1536-d).
Unless stated otherwise, targets are the 50-gene HEST HVG panel. The panel study additionally uses
matched 50-gene DEG (Wilcoxon selection across Leiden clusters) and HMHVG (mean-and-variability
selection after variance filtering) panels (Table~\ref{tab:panel_strategy}).

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
Intra-cohort training fits a model within each cohort. Pooled five-fold validation trains across
organs while holding out slide-level samples; organ identity defines the protocol but is not a model
input. LOOO trains on all-but-one organ and tests the held-out organ. We report PCC, SCC, and MSE
averaged over outer folds and three seeds. Checkpoints are selected on inner validation and evaluated
once on the outer test fold; single-slide folds use disjoint spots for inner validation. Patient-level
disjointness is not guaranteed where identifiers are unavailable.

\paragraph{Baselines and implementation.}
We compare with ST-Net \cite{he2020stnet}, Hist2ST \cite{zeng2022hist2st}, BLEEP \cite{xie2023bleep},
and STEM \cite{zhu2025diffusion} under matched features, panels, splits, budgets, and checkpoint
selection.
\morph{} uses four layers, width 256, four heads, eight neighbors, 16 radial bases, and 4.88M
parameters. AdamW training uses learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$, cosine decay,
gradient clipping, 100 epochs maximum, patience 20, and at most 3,000 spots per step.

\section{Computation Resources, Model Complexity, and Inference Scaling}
\label{app:compute}
All experiments ran on a shared GPU cluster. Feature extraction and training used NVIDIA GPUs with
compute capability $\geq$~sm\_80 (A100 80GB, A100-PCIe, L40s, and H200); Volta (V100) GPUs are
incompatible with our CUDA-13 / PyTorch build and were excluded.
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Encoders:} frozen UNI (ViT-L/16, 1024-d) and CONCH (512-d) patch features, concatenated
to 1536-d; no encoder fine-tuning.
\item \textbf{Model:} \morph{} has 4.88M trainable parameters (Table~\ref{tab:complexity}) and predicts
a whole slide in a single forward pass, with no iterative sampling.
\item \textbf{Training:} AdamW with a cosine schedule, 100 epochs with early stopping (patience~20),
nested validation, and a correlation-aware objective; three seeds per configuration.
\item \textbf{Inference:} single-pass; median model-only latency and peak memory versus slide size are
reported in Table~\ref{tab:scaling}, with feature extraction and disk I/O excluded.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mouse cross-organ diagnostic}
\label{app:mouse}
We evaluate 59 mouse Visium slides spanning seven organs with mouse-specific 50-gene panels. Pooled
performance reaches $0.887$, whereas every method remains near the floor under LOOO; we therefore do
not interpret the latter as evidence of successful cross-species organ transfer.
