\begin{abstract}
Predicting spatial gene expression from standard H\&E images could extend molecular analysis to tissues without spatial transcriptomic measurements. A key challenge is integrating local morphology with neighboring and tissue-wide context. We introduce \morph{}, a \textbf{M}ult\textbf{I}-context \textbf{S}patial \textbf{T}ransformer that fuses local \(k\)NN attention over spatial neighbors, global self-attention, and an attention-pooled slide summary in every layer to predict gene expression in a single forward pass. Its distance-based coordinate representation ensures invariance to translations, rotations, and reflections when image features are fixed and neighbor-distance ties are resolved consistently. In intra-cohort evaluation across six human organ cohorts from HEST-1k, \morph{} improves mean Pearson correlation over the strongest evaluated baseline by 9.7\%, 11.6\%, and 11.4\% for 10-, 50-, and 100-gene panels, respectively, while achieving the lowest average MSE and MAE. Gains persist across evaluated gene-selection strategies (HVG, DEG, and HMHVG) and image encoders (UNI, CONCH, and UNI+CONCH). In leave-one-organ-out evaluation, \morph{} improves mean Pearson correlation by 6.0\% over the next strongest baseline. It also attains the best expression-derived domain recovery (highest mean ARI and NMI at every clustering resolution, and higher NMI than the closest baseline on 84\% of held-out slides), while reproducing spatial expression patterns of selected cancer-marker genes. These results support multi-context integration as an effective approach to spatial gene expression prediction from histology.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) maps gene expression to tissue locations,
revealing spatial heterogeneity and tissue organization
\cite{staahl2016visualization}. However, its cost and experimental
requirements limit widespread use. Routine H\&E images contain
morphological signals associated with molecular state, motivating
prediction of spatial gene expression without matched ST measurements
\cite{chen2024stimage,jaume2024hest}.

A central challenge is integrating information from individual spots,
their neighborhoods, and the broader tissue. The methods evaluated
in this work approach prediction through different mechanisms:
ST-Net uses patch-level regression~\cite{he2020stnet},
Hist2ST combines transformer and graph-based modeling
\cite{zeng2022hist2st}, BLEEP uses cross-modal retrieval
\cite{xie2023bleep}, and STEM uses conditional diffusion
\cite{zhu2025diffusion}. These approaches motivate examining how
different forms of tissue context can be combined within a single
predictor. We therefore ask:

\begin{center}
\textit{\textbf{Can combining local spatial interactions,
spot-specific global interactions, and a shared slide summary
within every layer improve spatial gene expression prediction?}}
\end{center}

We introduce \morph{}, a multi-context spatial transformer that combines
these three messages in parallel within every layer
(Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). Starting from frozen UNI and/or CONCH embeddings
\cite{chen2024uni,lu2024conch}, local $k$NN attention aggregates
information from spatial neighbors, global self-attention allows each
spot to draw information from all spots, and attention pooling produces
a slide summary that is broadcast to every spot. The global and pooled
pathways serve different roles: global attention provides a
query-dependent representation for each spot, whereas pooling supplies
a shared representation of the slide. Their outputs are combined with
the local message and refined through residual feed-forward updates.
The resulting model predicts expression for all input spots in a
single forward pass. Coordinates enter \morph{} only to define the spatial $k$NN graph,
which is built from pairwise distances and is independent of the coordinate frame
\cite{satorras2021egnn}.
For fixed image features, deterministic evaluation, and consistently
resolved $k$NN distance ties, this construction yields invariance
to translations, rotations, and reflections in exact arithmetic.

We evaluate \morph{} on six human organ cohorts from
HEST-1k~\cite{jaume2024hest} under intra-cohort, pooled multi-organ,
and leave-one-organ-out settings. In intra-cohort evaluation,
\morph{} improves mean Pearson correlation over the strongest
evaluated baseline by 9.7\%, 11.6\%, and 11.4\% for 10-, 50-,
and 100-gene panels, respectively, with the lowest average MSE
and MAE. Gains persist across evaluated gene-selection strategies
and image encoders. In held-out-organ prediction, mean correlation
improves by 6.0\% over BLEEP~\cite{xie2023bleep}. On 58 held-out slides,
\morph{} attains the best expression-derived domain recovery (highest mean ARI and NMI at every
clustering resolution, and higher NMI than BLEEP on 84\% of slides), although absolute
agreement remains modest.

Our experiments address five research questions:
\textbf{RQ1 (Performance):} How accurately does \morph{} predict
gene expression under intra-cohort and pooled multi-organ training?
\textbf{RQ2 (Transfer):} How well does it generalize to an organ
excluded from training?
\textbf{RQ3 (Ablation):} How do the three context pathways and
spatial-neighborhood choices affect performance?
\textbf{RQ4 (Coordinate invariance):} How stable are predictions
under coordinate-frame transformations with image features fixed?
\textbf{RQ5 (Structure):} How well do predictions recover
expression-derived spatial domains?

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item We introduce a spatial transformer that integrates local
    attention, global self-attention, and pooled-slide context
    in parallel within every layer, and establish its
    coordinate-frame invariance under stated assumptions.

    \item We evaluate predictive performance across organs,
    gene panels, and image encoders under matched comparisons,
    including held-out-organ transfer and ablations of context
    pathways and spatial neighborhoods.

    \item We assess expression-derived domain recovery and
    selected cancer-marker expression patterns to examine
    biological structure beyond average prediction accuracy.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}

MIST maps frozen spot-level histology features and measured spot coordinates to a spatial gene
expression map. Its method has three stages: coordinate-invariant neighborhood construction, a shared
multi-context layer, and supervised prediction with a correlation-aware objective.

\subsection{Problem Setup}
For a slide with $N$ spots, let
$\mathbf{F}=[\mathbf{f}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{f}_N]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$
denote frozen pathology features and
$\mathbf{P}=[\mathbf{p}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{p}_N]^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times2}$ the corresponding
spot coordinates. The target
$\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times G}$ contains log-normalized expression for $G$ genes. We learn
$f_\theta:(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P})\mapsto\hat{\mathbf{Y}}$ and process each slide independently;
the model therefore supports a variable number of spots and does not require expression measurements at
test time. In the feature-matched main comparisons, $\mathbf{f}_i$ concatenates frozen UNI
\cite{chen2024uni} and CONCH \cite{lu2024conch} embeddings ($d_{\mathrm{in}}=1536$).


\subsection{Coordinate-Invariant Neighborhood Construction}
We compute pairwise Euclidean distances $D_{ij}=\lVert\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j\rVert_2$ and use
them to select the indices $\mathcal{N}(i)$ of each spot's $k$ nearest neighbors, excluding the spot
itself. Coordinates enter the model only through this neighborhood graph: distances are not used as
attention features or biases, and absolute positions are not added to spot embeddings. For any
orthogonal transformation $R$ and translation $\mathbf{t}$, the transformed coordinates
$\mathbf{p}'_i=R\mathbf{p}_i+\mathbf{t}$ preserve every pairwise Euclidean distance. Therefore, with
consistent tie handling, they produce the same $k$NN graph and the same predictions for fixed spot
features. The formal guarantee is given in Proposition~\ref{prop:coordinate_invariance}. This is
\emph{coordinate-frame} invariance of the spatial pathway given fixed spot features; it is distinct
from invariance to physically rotating or flipping the H\&E image, which would change the frozen
encoder's embeddings $\mathbf{F}$ and is outside this guarantee.

\subsection{Multi-Context MIST Layer}
\insightbox{Core Idea.}{At every layer, each spot receives three messages in parallel: one from its
nearby spots, one from all spots, and one from a pooled summary of the slide. The model adds these
messages to the current spot representation and refines the result with a feed-forward network.
Only the nearby-spot message uses coordinates, and only to select the local neighborhood.}

The input features are projected to hidden width $d$,
$\mathbf{X}^{(0)}=\mathbf{F}W_{\mathrm{in}}+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{in}}$. The same spatial neighbor graph
is reused in all $L$ transformer layers. At layer $\ell$, a pre-normalized
representation $\mathbf{H}=\operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)})$ is passed through three context
pathways.

\subsubsection{Local spatial-neighborhood attention}
Each spot attends to its $k$ neighbors using content-based attention. For head $h$, the attention
weights are
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij}^{h}=\operatorname{softmax}_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\!\left(
\frac{(\mathbf{q}_i^{h})^\top\mathbf{k}_j^{h}}{\sqrt{d_h}}\right),
\end{equation}
where $d_h=d/H$ for $H$ heads. The local output is the concatenation of the weighted value sums,
followed by a learned output projection. Thus, coordinates determine the local neighborhood, while
the attention weights depend on the spot representations.

\subsubsection{Global self-attention}
In parallel, standard multi-head self-attention
\cite{vaswani2017attention} allows every spot to attend to all $N$ spots. This pathway captures
long-range dependencies that may not be connected in the local spatial graph. It introduces no
direct coordinate-dependent bias and is permutation-equivariant with respect to spot order; in later
layers, its inputs may nevertheless carry spatial information propagated by the local and
slide-context pathways.

\subsubsection{Attention-pooled slide context}
A learned scalar scoring function produces
$a_i=\operatorname{softmax}_{i}(s(\mathbf{h}_i))$. The slide representation is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{g}=W_g\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_i\mathbf{h}_i\right)+\mathbf{b}_g,
\end{equation}
and is broadcast to every spot. Unlike a learned class token, this representation is computed
directly from the slide's current spot representations and summarizes the slide-level context.

For the complete model, the local ($\mathbf{L}$), global ($\mathbf{G}$), and broadcast slide
($\mathbf{S}$) representations are summed and added to the residual stream. A second
pre-normalization residual update applies a two-layer feed-forward network with expansion factor four
and GELU activation:
\begin{align}
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}
  &=\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{L}^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{G}^{(\ell)}+\mathbf{S}^{(\ell)},\\
\mathbf{X}^{(\ell+1)}
  &=\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}+
    \operatorname{FFN}\!\left(\operatorname{LN}(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)})\right).
\end{align}
After $L$ layers, layer normalization followed by a linear head maps each spot representation to its $G$ predicted gene values. Thus, \morph{} produces the complete slide-level gene-expression prediction in a single forward pass.


\par\noindent\refstepcounter{proposition}\textbf{Proposition \theproposition\ (Coordinate frame invariance):}\label{prop:coordinate_invariance}\par
For fixed model parameters and fixed histology features, in deterministic evaluation mode, let
$R\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ be orthogonal, let $\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$, and define
$\mathbf{P}'=\mathbf{P}R^\top+\mathbf{1}\mathbf{t}^\top$. If the $k$NN construction resolves distance
ties consistently, the prediction is unchanged:
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}')=f_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}).
\end{equation}

\par\noindent\refstepcounter{proposition}\textbf{Proposition \theproposition\ (Permutation equivariance):}\label{prop:permutation_equivariance}\par
Let $Q\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}$ be a permutation matrix. If the $k$NN construction handles distance
ties equivariantly, then
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(Q\mathbf{F},Q\mathbf{P})=Qf_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}).
\end{equation}
Therefore, reordering the input spots only reorders their predicted expression profiles.
Details, qualifications, and the proof for both of these propositions are provided in
Appendix~\ref{app:formal_guarantees}. Our numerical checks evaluate both
Propositions~\ref{prop:coordinate_invariance} and~\ref{prop:permutation_equivariance} under finite
precision.


\subsection{Training Objective and Ablations}
\label{sec:loss}
For a slide with measured expression $\mathbf{Y}$, we optimize mean squared error together with a
gene-wise Pearson-correlation term:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{MSE}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}},\mathbf{Y})
+ \lambda\left(1-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{V}}\rho_g\right),
\end{equation}
where $\rho_g$ is the Pearson correlation between predicted and measured expression across the $N$
spots, $\mathcal{V}$ is the set of genes with nonzero target variance on that slide, and
$\lambda=0.5$. MSE constrains the scale of the predictions, while the correlation term encourages
the model to reproduce spot-to-spot expression patterns.

The complete model uses all three context pathways. Their individual contributions are evaluated in
the factorial ablation study reported in Section~\ref{sec:design}, which compares local (L), global
(G), and slide-level (S) context configurations.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\noindent\textbf{Dataset and Image Encoder Selection.}\quad
We evaluate six HEST-1k~\cite{jaume2024hest} cohorts:
IDC (breast), CCRCC (kidney), LUNG (lung), PAAD (pancreas),
PRAD (prostate), and SKCM (skin). We concatenate frozen
UNI~\cite{chen2024uni} and CONCH~\cite{lu2024conch} embeddings,
which achieve the highest average predictive performance among
the evaluated encoder configurations. Dataset and encoder details
are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:extended_setup}.

\noindent\textbf{Gene-panel selection.}\quad
We use a 50-gene HVG panel by default and compare it with 50-gene
DEG and HMHVG panels (Table~\ref{tab:panel_strategy}).
HVG selection ranks genes by expression variance; DEG selection
uses differential expression across Leiden clusters; and HMHVG
selection combines mean expression and variability after variance
filtering. We also evaluate 10- and 100-gene HVG panels, and a
200-gene HVG panel used only for the cancer-marker case study
(Section~\ref{sec:casestudy}), since several literature markers lie
outside the smaller panels.
All selection steps use training data only. For each LOOO fold,
the panel is recomputed using the five training organs, excluding
expression data from the held-out organ.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation regimes.}\quad
We evaluate three settings:
\emph{intra-cohort}, with a separate model trained and tested within
each cohort;
\emph{pooled multi-organ}, with one model trained across organs
and tested on held-out slides from represented organs; and
\emph{leave-one-organ-out (LOOO)}, with training on five organs
and testing on the sixth.
Organ identity is not provided as an input.
All train--test splits are slide-disjoint. Because patient
identifiers are unavailable in our data pipeline, patient-level
disjointness cannot be verified; intra-cohort and pooled results
therefore measure slide-level generalization.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics and validation.}\quad
We report mean per-gene Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC)
as the primary metric, mean squared error (MSE) and mean absolute
error (MAE) for expression accuracy, and adjusted Rand index (ARI)
and normalized mutual information (NMI) for expression-derived
domain recovery. Checkpoints are selected using inner validation data and
evaluated once on the corresponding outer test fold. Results are
averaged across outer folds and three random seeds.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines and implementation.}\quad
We select one representative, publicly reproducible method per H\&E-to-ST modeling family: patch
regression (ST-Net~\cite{he2020stnet}), spatial graph modeling (Hist2ST~\cite{zeng2022hist2st}),
cross-modal retrieval (BLEEP~\cite{xie2023bleep}), and conditional generation
(STEM~\cite{zhu2025diffusion}). All are evaluated under matched features, panels,
splits, budgets, and model-selection rules. Unless otherwise stated, \morph{}
uses four layers, width 256, eight spatial neighbors, and 4.88M trainable
parameters. Full preprocessing, split, hyperparameter, and implementation
details are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:extended_setup}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{RQ1: Predictive performance across diverse organs}
\label{sec:rq1}

We first ask whether \morph{} consistently predicts gene expression across diverse organs. For a fair comparison, all methods use frozen UNI+CONCH features with matched gene panels and data splits.

We begin with \emph{within-organ evaluation}, where each model is trained and tested within the same organ. Across the 50-gene HVG, DEG, and HMHVG panels, \morph{} achieves mean PCCs of $0.496$, $0.615$, and $0.650$, respectively, compared with BLEEP's $0.444$, $0.559$, and $0.587$ (Table~\ref{tab:panel_strategy}). Across HVG panel sizes, \morph{} reaches PCC@10/50/100 of $0.600/0.496/0.559$, leading all six cohorts at PCC@50 with an $11.6\%$ cohort-averaged gain over BLEEP (Table~\ref{tab:main}). It also achieves the lowest average MSE and MAE across all evaluated panel strategies and sizes.

The improvement is consistent across runs. Treating each cohort--seed run as the statistical unit ($n{=}18$), \morph{} improves PCC@50 over BLEEP by $0.051$ (95\% bootstrap CI $[0.041,0.062]$; Wilcoxon $p=7.6\times10^{-6}$). At the gene level, \morph{} also achieves higher PCC than BLEEP for $87\%$ of the 300 evaluated genes; we report this descriptively because genes within a slide are correlated. Statistical details are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:significance}.

Finally, in the \emph{pooled multi-organ setting}, a single \morph{} model is trained jointly across all six organs using a shared 14-gene leakage-safe panel. It achieves the highest mean PCC ($0.599$), compared with BLEEP ($0.587$) and Hist2ST ($0.556$) (Table~\ref{tab:pooled6}). Together, these results show consistent gains both within individual organs and across a shared multi-organ setting.

\subsection{RQ3: Which components drive predictive accuracy?}
\label{sec:design}

A plain global-attention Transformer treats all spots interchangeably and
ignores where each spot sits in the tissue. Our premise is that a spot's
expression depends on tissue context at two scales this backbone does not model:
its immediate spatial neighborhood and the whole-slide state. We therefore add
two pathways on top of the vanilla backbone---a coordinate-invariant local
spatial-$k$NN pathway (\mbox{+Local}) and a slide-pooling pathway
(\mbox{+Slide})---and measure how much each contributes. We ablate on the pooled
(UNI+CONCH) and SKCM/LUNG intra-cohort (UNI) settings (Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}),
pairing runs by (setting, seed, fold) and testing each gap with a two-sided
Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

Adding local context improves over the vanilla backbone ($+0.016$, $p{=}0.02$),
and adding both pathways improves further and is best in every setting ($0.605$
pooled, $0.696$ SKCM, $0.589$ LUNG; $+0.024$ over the backbone,
$p{=}3.4\times10^{-4}$, $n{=}27$). The two pathways are complementary rather than
redundant: slide context does not help on its own ($-0.010$, $p{=}0.59$) but
adds a further $+0.007$ once layered on local context, and performance is
strongest when both are added. The local pathway draws on \emph{spatially}
defined neighbors, not merely extra attention: replacing $k$NN with random
neighbors of the same size costs $0.031$ PCC (Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}b;
$p{=}8.4\times10^{-5}$, $n{=}21$). Thus global attention is a capable backbone,
but the gains come from adding local and slide context on top of it.
Encoder-feature ablations are in Appendix~\ref{app:encoder}.

\section{Related Work}
\noindent\textbf{H\&E-to-ST prediction.}\quad Existing methods include patch-level regression
(ST-Net~\cite{he2020stnet}), spatial graph models (HisToGene~\cite{pang2021histogene};
Hist2ST~\cite{zeng2022hist2st}), and cross-modal retrieval (BLEEP~\cite{xie2023bleep}). STEM~\cite{zhu2025diffusion} instead models expression
conditionally with diffusion. Because published results use different features, panels, and splits,
we compare these model families under a matched protocol; \morph{} remains a deterministic point
predictor rather than a distributional model.

\noindent\textbf{Context modeling.}\quad Spatial predictors differ in how they aggregate context:
some use only local patches, whereas others add neighboring-spot graphs, nonlocal image information,
or multi-resolution features. \morph{} combines content-based attention over spatial-$k$NN neighbors,
standard self-attention over all spots, and an attention-pooled tissue summary in every layer.

\noindent\textbf{Pathology foundation models.}\quad Recent pathology encoders provide transferable
representations for morphology and tissue composition~\cite{chen2024uni,lu2024conch}. We use frozen
UNI and CONCH features as matched inputs for \morph{} and the baselines, and separately evaluate
encoder choice to distinguish representation quality from architectural effects.

\noindent\textbf{Geometry and generalization.}\quad Absolute position encodings can change when an
equivalent tissue layout is translated, rotated, or reflected; geometric networks instead construct
interactions from relative quantities~\cite{satorras2021egnn}. \morph{} uses pairwise Euclidean
distances, which are invariant to these frame changes for fixed histology features.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\morph{} is a coordinate-invariant spatial transformer that predicts gene expression from histology
by combining local $k$NN attention, global self-attention, and slide-level pooling in a single pass,
using pairwise distances so the spatial pathway is invariant to rigid coordinate transforms. Under
matched HEST-1k evaluation it gives the best mean intra-cohort correlation and shows modest but
consistent gains on unseen organs, though the pooled-to-LOOO gap shows anatomical transfer is still
the main open challenge for all methods. Natural next steps are conditioning on slide-level metadata
(e.g., organ or platform via FiLM) to close this gap, scaling toward transcriptome-wide targets,
end-to-end image encoding, and adding calibrated generative uncertainty.

\section{Formal Guarantees}
\label{app:formal_guarantees}

This section gives the detailed statements and proofs of the coordinate-frame invariance and
permutation-equivariance properties used in the main text. The guarantees concern deterministic
evaluation with fixed model parameters and fixed histology features. In finite-precision computation,
negligible round-off differences may occur; nearest-neighbor distance ties must also be resolved
consistently.

\subsection{Coordinate-frame invariance}

\textbf{Proposition 1 (Coordinate frame invariance).}
For fixed model parameters and fixed histology features, in deterministic evaluation mode, let
$R\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ be orthogonal, let $\mathbf{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$, and define
$\mathbf{P}'=\mathbf{P}R^\top+\mathbf{1}\mathbf{t}^\top$. If the $k$NN construction resolves distance
ties consistently, the prediction is unchanged:
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}')=f_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}).
\end{equation}

\textit{Proof.}
For any two spots,
\begin{align}
\mathbf{p}'_i-\mathbf{p}'_j
  &= (\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j)R^\top,\\
\|\mathbf{p}'_i-\mathbf{p}'_j\|_2^2
  &= (\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j)R^\top R(\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j)^\top
   = \|\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j\|_2^2.
\end{align}
Thus, the full distance matrix and the distance-based $k$NN ordering are unchanged, assuming
consistent tie handling. Any optional median-distance normalization is also unchanged because its
input distances are unchanged. The local attention receives the same neighbor indices, while the
global attention, slide pooling, residual blocks, and prediction head receive the same histology
features and intermediate representations. Induction over the transformer layers gives identical
outputs. The result does not assert invariance of the frozen image encoder to transformations of the
H\&E image itself. $\square$

\subsection{Permutation equivariance}

\textbf{Proposition 2 (Permutation equivariance).}
Let $Q\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}$ be a permutation matrix. If the $k$NN construction resolves distance
ties equivariantly, then
\begin{equation}
f_\theta(Q\mathbf{F},Q\mathbf{P})=Qf_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P}).
\end{equation}

\textit{Proof.}
Permuting the rows of $\mathbf{P}$ permutes the rows and columns of its pairwise distance matrix.
With equivariant tie handling, the neighbor list and corresponding distances are permuted in the
same way. Hence each local attention computation is permuted with its associated spot. The global
self-attention operation is permutation-equivariant because queries, keys, and values are permuted
together. Attention pooling produces the same pooled vector, since it is a weighted sum over the
spots, while broadcasting that vector preserves the row permutation. The input projection, residual
addition, layer normalization, feed-forward network, and final per-spot prediction head are all
row-wise equivariant. Applying these observations inductively through all layers yields
$f_\theta(Q\mathbf{F},Q\mathbf{P})=Qf_\theta(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{P})$. $\square$

\section{MIST Algorithm}
\label{app:algorithm}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:mist} gives the full forward pass corresponding to the Method:
coordinate-based $k$NN construction, $L$ multi-context layers that fuse local
$k$NN attention, global self-attention, and an attention-pooled slide token, and a linear gene head.
Coordinates enter only through the neighbor graph, which is computed once and reused in every layer;
local attention weights are content-based (no distance features or positional biases).

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\morph{} forward pass (one slide)}
\label{alg:mist}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require frozen features $\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$, coordinates
  $\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times2}$; layers $L$, neighbors $k$
\Ensure predicted expression $\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times G}$
\State $D_{ij}\gets \lVert \mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j\rVert_2$ \Comment{pairwise distances (frame-invariant)}
\State $\mathcal{N}(i) \gets k\text{NN}(D)$ \Comment{neighbor indices, self excluded (only use of coordinates)}
\State $\mathbf{X}\gets \mathbf{F}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}}+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{in}}$ \Comment{input projection to width $d$}
\For{$\ell=1$ \textbf{to} $L$}
  \State $\mathbf{H}\gets \operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{X})$
  \State $\mathbf{L}\gets \textsc{LocalKNNAttn}(\mathbf{H},\mathcal{N})$ \Comment{content-based attention over $k$ neighbors}
  \State $\mathbf{G}\gets \textsc{GlobalSelfAttn}(\mathbf{H})$ \Comment{full self-attention over all $N$ spots}
  \State $a_i\gets \operatorname{softmax}_i(s(\mathbf{h}_i))$;\quad
         $\mathbf{g}\gets \mathbf{W}_g\!\sum_i a_i\mathbf{h}_i+\mathbf{b}_g$ \Comment{attention-pooled slide token}
  \State $\mathbf{X}\gets \mathbf{X}+\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{1}\mathbf{g}^\top$ \Comment{parallel fusion + residual}
  \State $\mathbf{X}\gets \mathbf{X}+\operatorname{FFN}(\operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{X}))$
\EndFor
\State $\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}\gets \operatorname{LN}(\mathbf{X})\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{head}}+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{head}}$
\State \Return $\widehat{\mathbf{Y}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent Training minimizes
$\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{MSE}(\widehat{\mathbf{Y}},\mathbf{Y})+\lambda\big(1-\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{V}}\rho_g\big)$
with $\lambda{=}0.5$, where $\rho_g$ is the per-gene Pearson correlation over spots and $\mathcal{V}$
is the set of genes with nonzero target variance on the slide.

\section{Extended Experimental Setup}
\label{app:extended_setup}

\paragraph{Dataset and cohort selection.}
We use \textbf{HEST-1k}~\cite{jaume2024hest}, a collection of spatial-transcriptomics
measurements paired with matched H\&E whole-slide images, available from the
\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/MahmoodLab/hest}{HEST-1k Hugging Face repository}. HEST-1k has
supported subsequent histology-to-transcriptomics studies, including STEM and STPath
\cite{zhu2025diffusion,stpath2025}. Our experiments use six cohorts: CCRCC, IDC, LUNG, PAAD, PRAD,
and SKCM. Appendix~\ref{app:cohort_stats} reports their organs, sample and spot counts, and fold
counts.

\paragraph{Image encoders.}
We use frozen UNI (ViT-L/16, 1,024-dimensional image embeddings;
\href{https://huggingface.co/MahmoodLab/UNI}{Hugging Face model card})~\cite{chen2024uni} and
CONCH (ViT-B/16, 512-dimensional image embeddings;
\href{https://huggingface.co/MahmoodLab/CONCH}{Hugging Face model card})~\cite{lu2024conch}.
Both encoders were evaluated for gene-expression prediction in the HEST-1k benchmark
\cite{jaume2024hest}. We test UNI and CONCH separately and concatenate their features to obtain
1,536-dimensional spot embeddings. Concatenation gives the highest mean PCC in our encoder
comparison: 0.496, compared with 0.484 for UNI and 0.463 for CONCH alone (Table~\ref{tab:main};
Appendix~\ref{tab:encoder}). We therefore use UNI+CONCH in the main comparisons.

\paragraph{Preprocessing and gene panels.}
We use the provided slide-level cross-validation folds and transform expression targets with $\log(1+x)$.
Unless stated otherwise, targets are the 50-gene HEST HVG panel. The panel study additionally uses
matched 50-gene DEG, selected by Wilcoxon testing across Leiden clusters, and HMHVG panels, selected
using mean-expression and variability criteria after variance filtering (Table~\ref{tab:panel_strategy}).

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
Intra-cohort training fits a model within each cohort. Pooled five-fold validation trains across
organs while holding out slide-level samples; organ identity defines the protocol but is not a model
input. LOOO trains on all-but-one organ and tests the held-out organ. We report PCC, MSE, MAE, and
(for domain recovery) ARI and NMI, averaged over outer folds and three seeds. Checkpoints are selected on inner validation and evaluated
once on the outer test fold; single-slide folds use disjoint spots for inner validation. Patient-level
disjointness is not guaranteed where identifiers are unavailable.

\paragraph{Baselines and implementation.}
We compare with ST-Net \cite{he2020stnet}, Hist2ST \cite{zeng2022hist2st}, BLEEP \cite{xie2023bleep},
and STEM \cite{zhu2025diffusion} under matched features, panels, splits, budgets, and checkpoint
selection. All methods use the \emph{same} fold assignments, the same frozen UNI+CONCH inputs, and the
same three random seeds ($1,2,3$); every method is trained per cohort/regime and its checkpoint is
selected by best inner-validation PCC, then evaluated once on the outer test fold. \morph{} uses four
layers, width 256, four heads, eight neighbors, and 4.88M parameters.
AdamW training uses learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$, cosine decay, gradient clipping,
100 epochs maximum, patience 20, and at most 3,000 spots per step.

\paragraph{System and software environment.}
All experiments run on Linux (RHEL~9, kernel~5.14) with Python~3.13 and PyTorch~2.12 (CUDA~13.0,
cuDNN~9.2). Core scientific dependencies are NumPy~2.4, SciPy~1.17, scikit-learn~1.8, pandas~2.3, and
scanpy~1.12. Frozen UNI and CONCH features are extracted with \texttt{timm} and the official model
weights. Training and inference use NVIDIA GPUs with compute capability $\geq$~sm\_80 (A100~80GB,
A100-PCIe, L40s, and H200; Appendix~\ref{app:compute}); Volta (V100) GPUs are incompatible with this
CUDA-13 build and were excluded. Cluster nodes use Intel Xeon Gold~6140 CPUs.

\paragraph{Split granularity and patient disjointness.}
Each ``sample'' in Table~\ref{tab:cohort_stats} is a distinct HEST-1k slide (a whole-slide section),
and every fold is \emph{slide-disjoint}: no slide is shared between training and test, the pooled
regime holds out entire slides, and the leave-one-organ-out regime holds out entire organs. HEST-1k
patient/donor identifiers are not distributed with the per-slide data in our pipeline, so for a cohort
in which one donor may contribute several slides we cannot verify patient-level disjointness. The
intra-cohort and pooled numbers should therefore be read as \emph{slide-level} generalization and
could be optimistic if a cohort mixes multiple slides from the same patient; this risk is largest for
the two multi-slide cohorts, CCRCC (24 slides) and PRAD (23 slides), and small for the remaining
cohorts (IDC~4, PAAD~3, LUNG~2, SKCM~2 slides). Importantly, the cross-organ LOOO transfer results
(our central generalization claim) are unaffected, because an entire organ (and hence a disjoint set
of patients) is withheld at test time.

\section{Computation Resources, Model Complexity, and Inference Scaling}
\label{app:compute}
All experiments ran on a shared GPU cluster. Feature extraction and training used NVIDIA GPUs with
compute capability $\geq$~sm\_80 (A100 80GB, A100-PCIe, L40s, and H200); Volta (V100) GPUs are
incompatible with our CUDA-13 / PyTorch build and were excluded.
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Encoders:} frozen UNI (ViT-L/16, 1024-d) and CONCH (512-d) patch features, concatenated
to 1536-d; no encoder fine-tuning.
\item \textbf{Model:} \morph{} has 4.88M trainable parameters (Table~\ref{tab:complexity}) and predicts
a whole slide in a single forward pass, with no iterative sampling.
\item \textbf{Training:} AdamW with a cosine schedule, 100 epochs with early stopping (patience~20),
nested validation, and a correlation-aware objective; three seeds per configuration.
\item \textbf{Inference:} single-pass; median model-only latency and peak memory versus slide size are
reported in Table~\ref{tab:scaling}, with feature extraction and disk I/O excluded.
\end{itemize}
